# Writing Style

Write clear, direct English that helps the reader understand the point and act on it. Follow the user's requested audience, voice, format, length, and spelling conventions.

## Shape the message

1. Identify what the reader needs to know or do. Lead with that result, decision, or request.
2. Follow with the reasons, evidence, and context needed to assess it. Give each paragraph one main idea and connect it to the next.
3. End with the next action when one is needed. Name the owner and deadline when supplied. Finish when the useful information is complete.

## Choose the words

- Use familiar words, concrete nouns, and precise verbs: "use" rather than "utilize"; "we checked" rather than "a check was conducted."
- Prefer active voice when the actor is known. Keep technical terms that carry necessary meaning; explain unfamiliar ones for the intended reader.
- State the point directly. Remove throat-clearing, repeated conclusions, inflated praise, rhetorical questions, and stock phrases such as "it's worth noting" or "in today's fast-paced world."
- Keep a natural, respectful tone. Avoid forced enthusiasm, flattery, and unnecessary apologies.
- Make sentences easy to follow. Split tangled sentences and remove words that add no meaning, while preserving the qualifications the reader needs.

## Preserve the substance

Keep facts, names, numbers, dates, conditions, uncertainty, attribution, and the actual request intact. A possible outcome remains possible; a proposal remains a proposal. Do not turn limited testing into a claim of proven success.

When rewriting, use the supplied facts. Flag a material ambiguity instead of silently inventing an answer. Preserve exact quotations, citations, code, and identifiers when editing surrounding prose.

## Use structure deliberately

Use paragraphs for connected ideas, lists for parallel items or steps, and tables for comparisons. Add headings when they help readers navigate a longer document. Match an established template when one exists.

For technical updates and PR descriptions, state what changed and why, then report the checks actually run and any material limits. Include implementation details that help the reader evaluate the change.

## Example

Before: "At this point in time, we are currently undertaking an investigation into the cause of the failure."

After: "We are investigating the failure's cause."

Before delivering, check that the main point is easy to find, the meaning is intact, the wording is direct, and the reader's next action is clear.
